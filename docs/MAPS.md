# Building a stage

Everything you need to lay out a map that plays well, in the order you need it.
Read §1 and §2 once; after that §2 is the only part you come back to.

Stages are **code**, not editor-authored: a stage is a list of `Rect2` plus a
handful of placed terrain elements. That sounds worse than it is — the whole
layout is thirty lines you can retune between runs without opening a scene — but
it does mean the loop is *edit, run, look*. §5 is how to make that loop fast.

---

## 1. The grid

- **Tile = 16 px.** Every number below is in pixels; divide by 16 for tiles.
- **Origin is top-left.** `y` grows **downward**. A platform at `y=304` is
  *above* one at `y=496`.
- **A body is 22 × 34 px.** Standing, its centre sits **24 px above** whatever it
  is standing on — so a spawn on a surface whose top edge is `y=496` goes at
  `y=472`.
- Crouched, the body is **17 px** tall. That is what a low corridor has to clear.
- `Rect2(x, y, w, h)` is **top-left corner** plus size. `Rect2(496, 288, 160, 16)`
  is a 10-tile ledge whose walkable surface is at `y=288`.

Stage sizes (DESIGN 6.1): Small ~40×24 tiles, Medium ~56×32, Large ~72×40.
Rooftop Rumble is Large (72×40) because it doubles as the movement test bench;
Cryo Lab is Medium (56×32).

## 2. The reach envelope — the numbers that decide everything

Generated by flying the real body through the real state machine:

```bash
~/Downloads/Godot_v4.7.1-stable_win64.exe/Godot_v4.7.1-stable_win64_console.exe --headless --path . tools/measure_reach.tscn
```

**Re-run it after any movement tune.** These are not derived from the config by
arithmetic — they account for the boost window, the momentum cap, air control and
frame granularity, which is the difference between a 130 px gap and a coin flip.

### Vertical — how high the next surface can be

| | Reach | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Tapped hop | **24 px** (1.5t) | The smallest deliberate step up. |
| Held jump | **92 px** (5.7t) | **The number.** A ledge higher than this off flat ground is unreachable without a dash. |
| Jump + up-dash | **103 px** (6.4t) | The hard ceiling from flat ground, and it costs a dash charge. |

So: **stack platforms ≤ 92 px apart** if you want them freely reachable; put
something at 92–103 px when you want it to cost a dash; above 103 px it needs a
wall, a pole, or a spring.

### Horizontal — how wide a gap can be

| | Reach | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Held jump, standing | **63 px** (4.0t) | From a standstill. |
| Held jump, base run | **113 px** (7.0t) | A short run-up. |
| Held jump, capped run | **133 px** (8.3t) | Full momentum, no dash. |
| Slide jump, capped run | **203 px** (12.7t) | Low arc — needs headroom clearance, not height. |
| Capped jump + air dash | **275 px** (17.2t) | The widest gap worth building. |

**A gap over ~133 px demands a dash or a slide jump.** Over 275 px it is a wall,
not a gap — which is fine if that is what you meant.

### Dashes

| | Reach |
|---|---|
| Ground dash | **95 px** (5.9t) |
| Air dash, sideways | **91 px** (5.7t) |
| Air dash, straight down | **15 px** (0.9t) — deliberately gutted; diagonals are not |

### Walls

| | Value |
|---|---|
| First wall jump, rise | **42 px** (2.6t) |
| Same-wall repeat, rise | **1 px** — chains on one wall barely climb, by design |
| Wall dash along a face | **48 px** (3.0t) |

**A climbable shaft needs two facing walls.** Moving to a different face resets
the chain to full impulse; hammering the same face does not (DESIGN 4.4). A
single tall wall is a dead end, and that is a legitimate thing to build — just
know you are building it.

Known-good shaft widths: **64 px** (Rooftop Rumble — narrow enough that two
players climbing it meet, which is how wall-jump duels happen on purpose) and
**128 px** (Cryo Lab — climbable without forcing contact).

## 3. The rules you cannot break

From CLAUDE.md and DESIGN §6.1. These are not style preferences:

1. **Sealed box, no pits.** Always start `arena_blocks()` from
   `Arena.sealed_box(arena_size())`. Nothing may kill by falling, because
   nothing except a stomp may kill at all.
2. **Symmetric spawns on opposite sides.** `spawns()` returns **one anchor per
   team**; the base spreads teammates around it, so you never place six points.
3. **Terrain can stun, push, launch and redirect — never take a life.** The
   terrain harness asserts this for every element.
4. **Leave one clear runway.** Momentum is the game. Rooftop Rumble's street runs
   the full width with nothing in the jump arc, and that emptiness is most of why
   the stage is that big. A stage with no runway plays like a different game.

## 4. The pieces you have

### Terrain elements (`src/stage/terrain/`)

| Element | Key params | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| `Pole` | `size` | Grab, climb, leap off. Zeroes momentum and **refills the dash** — a reset button. Put one where someone who just got chased across the map wants it. |
| `Ice` | `size`, `slip` | Near-zero friction. Somewhere to *build* speed, not only lose control. Applies while standing on it, so the area must overlap the body. |
| `StunLine` | `size`, `cycle_time`, `on_ratio`, `phase_offset`, `warn_time` | Tripwire. Stuns, keeps momentum, **leaves the head hurtbox live** — a stomp setup, not a shield. With `cycle_time > 0` it becomes a timed laser; stagger `phase_offset` across several to make a grid a rhythm. |
| `JumpSpring` | `size`, `launch_velocity` | Vertical launch. Keeps horizontal speed. Narrow ones get aimed at; wide ones get fallen onto. |
| `SpeedPad` | `size`, `direction`, `boost_speed` | Sets you at or over the momentum cap along its direction. |
| `Portal` | `size`, `linked_portal`, `facing` | Paired teleport that **rotates the velocity vector** into the exit's facing. Redirects momentum without giving or taking any. |
| `WindZone` | `size`, `force` | Constant push. Rooftop's updraft does not beat gravity — it makes a shaft climbable *with* wall jumps rather than climbing it for you. |
| `ExplosionHazard` | `size`, `period`, `warning_time` | Periodic blast: shove plus a brief stun, on a telegraph. |

All of them are `TerrainElement`s: set `size`, set `position` (that is the
**centre**, not a corner), `add_child` them in `build_terrain()`.

### Art

`assets/stages/tile_*.png` — one 16 px tile per element, plus `tile_ground.png`
(Rooftop's brick) and `tile_lab.png` (Cryo's steel plate). Add a new one in
`assets/tools/generate_placeholders.py` and re-run it; see `assets/STYLE_GUIDE.md`
for the palette.

Your stage picks its tile via `ground_texture()`, its backdrop via `sky_bands()`
+ `horizon()` + `ground_fill()`, and its surface highlight via `cap_color()`.

## 5. The loop: how to actually iterate

### Press F3 in any stage

That is the design overlay (`src/ui/stage_grid.gd`):

- **Tile grid** with coordinates labelled every 128 px — so "somewhere left of
  the slab" becomes a number you can paste into `arena_blocks()`.
- **Spawn markers** with their exact coordinates and a body-sized box, for every
  seat in the current format.
- **The reach envelope drawn around player 1** — horizontal lines at the 92 px
  jump apex and 103 px jump+dash ceiling, vertical lines at the 133 px and 275 px
  gap limits. Walk to a ledge and you can see whether the next one is reachable
  without doing arithmetic.

The overlay's numbers come from `tools/measure_reach.gd`. If you tune movement,
re-run the tool and update the constants at the top of `stage_grid.gd` together —
otherwise the overlay starts lying.

### Iterate on geometry, not on the game

`F6` in the Godot editor with a stage scene open boots straight into it, skipping
the lobby and select screens. Change a `Rect2`, F6, look, repeat. That is the
fastest loop available.

## 6. Making a new stage

1. **Script** in `src/stage/`, extending `MatchStage`. Override only:
   `stage_id()`, `arena_size()`, `spawns()`, `arena_blocks()`, `build_terrain()`,
   and the palette hooks. Do **not** copy the round loop — the base owns it.
2. **Scene** — copy `src/stage/cryo_lab.tscn`, repoint the script, move the
   Camera2D to the centre of your arena and set its zoom to
   `viewport / arena size` (1280÷896 ≈ 1.4 for a 56-tile stage).
3. **Register** it in `GameManager.STAGE_ROSTER` with `scene`, `name`, `blurb`,
   `features`, `accent`. The select screen reads all five and never instantiates
   the stage.
4. **Cover it** in `tests/terrain_harness.gd` — copy `_check_cryo_lab`: assert
   the box is sealed, that living in it for a few cycles costs no life, and that
   the elements you placed are actually there.
5. **Document** it: a row in DESIGN §6.3, and IMPLEMENTATION §3a if you changed
   any `MatchStage` hook.

Then run all four harnesses before committing.

## 6b. A worked build from a sketch — Sunken Court

`src/stage/sunken_court.gd` was built from a hand sketch, and every distance in it
was chosen against §2 rather than eyeballed:

- **Trench 128 px deep** — chosen to be *more* than the 92 px held jump, so you
  cannot climb out unaided. That is the whole premise of the stage.
- **Trench 224 px wide** — too wide to wall-jump across without losing height
  (a kick gives 42 px of rise and ~126 px of push), which is what stops the
  facing walls from quietly undoing the depth.
- **Platform 80 px above the mesa tops** — *inside* the 92 px held jump, so the
  high ground is reachable from either side without spending a dash, but only if
  you commit to the jump.
- **Springs at −820** — lift a body ~177 px, which clears the 128 px lip
  comfortably but stops 26 px short of the platform's underside. The floor gets
  you out of the hole; it does not hand you the high ground.
- **Poles hanging to y=280** — a held jump from a mesa apexes at y=252, so the
  pole is passed on the way up and grabbed in flight.

The one thing the sketch could not tell me, and testing did: springs spanning the
trench wall-to-wall meant a 22 px body always touched one, so you could never
*stand* in the trench at all — it was a bounce pad, not a place. 32 px of plain
floor against each wall fixed it. That is the kind of thing only the harness or
your hands will catch, which is why §6 step 4 exists.

## 7. Worked example — why Rooftop Rumble is shaped like that

Read `src/stage/duel.gd` alongside this.

- **The street** runs the full 72-tile width with nothing on it. B-hop chains
  need a runway with no geometry in the arc. This is the single biggest reason
  the stage is Large rather than the Small a 1v1 calls for.
- **Rooftops are slabs with air beneath**, not solid buildings — that keeps the
  street open underneath. In a stomp game you want vertical layers you can pass
  between, not walls.
- **The shaft is 64 px wide** with facing walls at `x=528` and `x=592`. Bodies
  are 22 px, so two players climbing it are forced into contact — that is how
  wall-jump duels (DESIGN 3.4) happen deliberately instead of by accident.
- **The high slab spans the shaft** (`Rect2(496, 288, 160, 16)`), so the most
  valuable platform is directly above the most contested route.
- **Every terrain element sits clear of the street** — putting anything on the
  runway would take away the one part of the stage that is deliberately empty.
- **Stepping platforms at `y=496`** sit 96 px above the awnings and 208 px below
  the rooftops: reachable by jump from below, and a dash-or-nothing from the
  street. Compare those to the table in §2 and you can see the intent.

The thing to copy is not the shapes — it is that every distance in that file was
chosen against §2 and every element placement was chosen against §3.4.
